Global Hazard Load (0–100)

Global Hazard Load represents the total physical energy currently straining our planet. Instead of counting raw events, it weights them by destructive potential:

Depth-Damped Tremors (Up to 65%): Shallow quakes release violence directly at the surface; deep tremors dissipate before reaching people. The engine mathematically damps every quake by its hypocenter depth.
Active Cyclonic Storms (Up to 15%): Tracks massive low-pressure vortices actively spinning over warm oceans toward populated coastlines.
Space Weather & Geomagnetic Storms (Up to 15%): Tracks solar coronal mass ejections that bend Earth’s magnetic field and threaten electrical grids and satellites.

Hazard Velocity (Rate of Change)

Velocity does not measure how many hazards exist—it measures how fast new hazards are accelerating right now compared to yesterday:

• Positive Velocity (+%): Disasters are emerging or intensifying faster in the last 6 hours than in the preceding 18 hours. This signals sudden swarms or rapid storm formation.
• Negative Velocity (-%): Activity is calming down and falling below the 24-hour moving average.

Hazard Convergence Explained

Disasters become catastrophic when multiple threats strike the same area at once. Hazard Convergence answers three simple questions:

1. Cluster Pressure (--%)
Are multiple earthquakes grouping together in the same 350 km region? Swarms show the earth's crust is actively bending and shifting stress to nearby faults.
2. Overdue Fault Risk (--%)
Did this tremor happen near a major locked fault line (like the Himalayas or San Andreas) that hasn't ruptured in centuries and has massive pent-up energy?
3. Compound Threat (--%)
Are multiple different disasters overlapping? (e.g., an earthquake hitting an area drenched by cyclone rain triggers catastrophic landslides).

Evacuation Runway & Safety Protocol

The Evacuation Runway is the countdown clock to severe landfall. When a tropical cyclone approaches, it gives teams and families an exact action window before roads flood and power cuts.

Standard Operating Procedures by Runway:
• 48 Hours Remaining: Stage emergency backup power; verify satellite communications; review supply line diversions.
• 24 Hours Remaining: Secure outdoor cranes, storage containers, and loose roof panels; evacuate low-lying coastal staff.
• 12 Hours Remaining: Safe shutdown of plant machinery and electrical substations; complete final inland relocation.

Inside Ground Motion (MMI ≥ IV)

Most platforms multiply total quakes by an arbitrary number. The Brink Engine does not fake population exposure.

How Real Exposure Is Calculated:
1. Physical Shaking Radius: Uses the standard geophysical equation $R = 10^{0.433 \cdot M - 0.82}$ to calculate how far shaking travels before fading below felt threshold (MMI IV).
2. Depth Damping: Deep quakes (e.g. 150 km) lose energy traveling up through bedrock and expose fewer people.
3. Spatial Density Grid: An earthquake in the open ocean exposes 0 people. An earthquake beneath an urban industrial corridor calculates real human density.
